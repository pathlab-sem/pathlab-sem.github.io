CB-SEM

Covariance-based structural equation modelling and confirmatory factor analysis with lavaan and semTools, drawn on the same canvas as PLS-SEM.

  10. Step 8. Compare with the PLS-SEM results
  11. Step 9. Report the results

Step by step

  1. Draw the model on the canvas, or send a PLS-SEM model to CB-SEM from the analysis tab menu.
  2. Choose the estimator and how missing values are handled.
  3. Click Estimate model.
  4. Check model fit first, then loadings, reliability and the structural paths.

What you get

  • Chi-square, CFI, TLI, RMSEA and SRMR
  • Standardised and unstandardised estimates with standard errors
  • Reliability, AVE and discriminant validity
  • Modification indices and measurement invariance across groups

Getting your data in

This analysis uses the project dataset. If the project has no data yet, upload a file on the Data tab (drop it there or click Choose file). If the project already has data, they are used at once.

  1. Build the model from the indicators, as in PLS-SEM, or send an existing PLS-SEM model to CB-SEM.
  2. Choose listwise deletion or FIML for missing values in the CB-SEM settings.

CB-SEM needs a larger sample than PLS-SEM; 200 or more respondents is a common guide.

Practical demonstration: confirming the model with CB-SEM

This example continues the technology adoption study from the PLS-SEM page (320 respondents, six constructs). CB-SEM first tests the measurement model with a confirmatory factor analysis (CFA), then the structural model, and the results are compared with PLS-SEM.

CB-SEM treats every construct as a common factor: its items are assumed to reflect it. Keep this in mind for Facilitating Conditions, whose items are formative (Mode B) in the PLS-SEM model.

Step 1. Send the PLS-SEM model to CB-SEM

  1. Right-click the tab of the PLS-SEM analysis and choose Send to CB-SEM….
  2. Read the check: every construct becomes a common factor, and Facilitating Conditions is flagged because its items were formative.
  3. Create the CB-SEM analysis. It opens as a CFA: the six factors all covary, and the structural paths are not copied yet.

Step 2. Choose the estimator and the missing-data treatment

  1. Click Analysis settings in the Properties panel to open the CB-SEM settings.
  2. Keep Maximum likelihood (ML) for roughly normal data; choose Robust ML (MLR) for non-normal data, or WLSMV for ordinal items.
  3. Under Missing values, choose Full information ML (FIML): it keeps all 320 respondents instead of dropping the 8 with a missing answer.

Step 3. Estimate the CFA and check the model fit

Click Run (Estimate model) and open the Model fit tab. Judge the fit with several indices together, not with one alone.

IndexGood fitIn the example
Chi-square / dfBelow 3169.18 / 137 = 1.24
CFI and TLI0.95 or more (0.90 acceptable)CFI 0.986, TLI 0.983
RMSEA0.06 or less (0.08 acceptable)0.027, 90% CI 0.009 to 0.040
SRMR0.08 or less0.041

The CFA fits well (Hu and Bentler, 1999). The chi-square test is significant (p = 0.032), which is common with a few hundred respondents; the other indices carry more weight.

Read the messages above the table. Here, many bootstrap runs gave non-admissible solutions: a first sign that one factor (Facilitating Conditions) does not hold together.

Step 4. Check the measurement model

Open the Measurement tab: it lists the standardised loadings and, for each factor, the reliability (omega) and the average variance extracted (AVE).

  • Five factors are reliable and valid: omega 0.83 to 0.87 and AVE 0.58 to 0.70 (thresholds 0.70 and 0.50).
  • Facilitating Conditions fails: omega 0.20, AVE 0.08, loadings 0.16 to 0.36. Its three items do not reflect one common factor.

This is the formative construct of the PLS-SEM model. Do not force it into a common factor: keep it as a Mode B composite in PLS-SEM, drop it from the CB-SEM model, or report CB-SEM results for it with this limitation.

Step 5. Check discriminant validity

Open the Discriminant validity tab. All HTMT ratios are below 0.85; the highest is 0.60, between Effort Expectancy and Facilitating Conditions. The factors measure different things.

Step 6. Add the structural paths and estimate the full model

  1. Once the CFA is acceptable, press P (the Path tool) and draw the six hypothesised paths, as in the PLS-SEM model.
  2. Click Run again and open the Structural tab: each path has its standardised estimate, standard error, z and p value.
PathStandardised βp

The full model also fits well: chi-square (143) = 181.29, CFI = 0.983, TLI = 0.980, RMSEA = 0.029, SRMR = 0.046. The model explains 19% of the variance of performance expectancy, 22% of behavioural intention and 28% of use behaviour.

Step 7. Look at the modification indices, with care

The Modification indices tab lists parameters that would improve the fit if they were freed. Here the largest is 8.6 (PE4 also loading on Behavioural Intention): small, and with no theoretical reason, so the model is kept as it is.

Free a parameter only when theory supports it, and report every change. Changing the model until it fits capitalises on chance.

Step 8. Compare with the PLS-SEM results

Open Compare with PLS: the paths of both analyses sit side by side. Five of the six paths lead to the same conclusion. Facilitating Conditions to Use Behaviour differs: significant in PLS-SEM (p < 0.001), not in CB-SEM (p = 0.061), because the construct is a composite in one and a weak common factor in the other.

Step 9. Report the results

Open the Report tab of the CB-SEM analysis for the complete report and its references. Typical sentences, written from the example:

A confirmatory factor analysis (ML, FIML, n = 320) showed good fit: χ²(137) = 169.18, p = .032, CFI = .986, TLI = .983, RMSEA = .027, 90% CI [.009, .040], SRMR = .041 (Hu & Bentler, 1999).

In the structural model (CFI = .983, RMSEA = .029, SRMR = .046), effort expectancy predicted performance expectancy (β = .43, p < .001), which predicted behavioural intention (β = .42, p < .001); behavioural intention predicted use behaviour (β = .43, p < .001).

To compare groups such as men and women, open the Invariance tab: measurement invariance must hold before group means or paths are compared.

Good to know

  • CFI and TLI of 0.95 or more and RMSEA up to 0.06 indicate good fit (Hu and Bentler, 1999).
  • Use measurement invariance before comparing groups.

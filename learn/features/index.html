Feature highlights

A tour of the features that save the most time: moving a model between PLS-SEM, CB-SEM and GSCA, construct scores in two clicks, filters and transforms without formulas, drawing a QCA model on a canvas, path lines shaped like vector drawings, a test proposed for your question, and the study workflow figure and integrated findings table for your paper.

One model, three methods: PLS-SEM, CB-SEM and GSCA

Draw your model once and take it to the method your reviewers ask for. PathLab copies it into a new analysis of the other type, so both versions stay in the project side by side, with the same data.

  1. Right-click the tab of a PLS-SEM analysis, above the canvas.
  2. Choose Send to CB-SEM… or Duplicate as GSCA.
  3. For CB-SEM, a check lists what happens to each construct before anything is created. Click Create CB-SEM analysis.
  4. The CB-SEM copy starts as a confirmatory factor analysis of the measurement model. Once it fits, draw the structural paths in CB-SEM and estimate the full model.
  5. GSCA copies the whole model, structural paths included, ready to run.

If you change the original model later, the copy shows a banner and can be updated, so the two versions do not drift apart.

Bulk compute: construct scores in two clicks

Scale scores are needed everywhere: for descriptives, regression, latent profiles, fsQCA or machine learning. Bulk compute makes all of them at once instead of one formula at a time.

  1. Open the Data tab and choose Data preview, then click Bulk compute.
  2. PathLab proposes the groups of items: the constructs of your models, and items named like PE1, PE2, PE3, PE4.
  3. Choose the function, for example the mean or the sum, and tick the groups you want.
  4. Click Create: each score becomes a new variable, rounded to 3 decimals, available in every analysis.

Bulk compute is also in the Machine learning ribbon and in the Statistics Data menu (Bulk computed variable). For anything else, write a formula instead.

Transform and filter in Statistics, without formulas

The Statistics analysis has jamovi-style data tools on its Data tab. They never change the uploaded file: filters and transformed variables are computed columns that update by themselves.

  • Filters: keep only the rows that meet plain conditions, such as gender is Female or age is between 18 and 35. The rows kept are counted at once, and Edit as formula shows the same filter as a formula.
  • Transform: recode a variable with reusable rules (if the value is … use …, otherwise …). The transformed variable sits next to its source and follows every change.
  • Several variables can share one transform, for example to reverse-code all negatively worded items at once.
  1. In the Statistics analysis, open the Data tab.
  2. Click Filters in the ribbon and add a condition, or right-click a variable and choose Transform….
  3. Run any analysis: it uses the filtered rows and the transformed variables straight away.

The ribbon can be collapsed to give the data and the results the whole window: click the caret at the end of the tab row, double-click a tab, or press Ctrl+F1. While it is collapsed, clicking a tab shows that ribbon over the work for one command; the pin brings it back.

The ribbon never scrolls sideways. In a narrow window the groups at the right fold into a single button, as in Office; click one to open the whole group, and widen the window to lay them out in full again.

Draw your QCA model on a canvas

QCA can be set up visually, the way SEM models are drawn. The drawing and the form edit the same model, so you can switch between them at any time.

  1. Open the QCA analysis and choose Draw at the top of the workspace.
  2. Drag the variable you want to explain into the circle on the right: it becomes the outcome.
  3. Drag the conditions into the conditions area, or use the + buttons in the variable list. The arrows are drawn for you.
  4. Click a variable in the drawing to set up its calibration, then run the truth table.

After a run, a solution pathway can be laid over the drawing: present, absent and does-not-matter conditions are shown on the arrows.

Study workflow figure: your methods as a flowchart

Multi-method papers usually need a figure of the analytical process. PathLab draws it from the analyses in your project: data preparation on top, one coloured stage per analysis (PLS-SEM, NCA, fsQCA, SEM-ANN and others) with its real settings, and the checks that send you back when they fail.

  1. Run your analyses first, for example a PLS-SEM model with NCA switched on and an fsQCA analysis created from it.
  2. Click Export in the toolbar and choose Study workflow figure.
  3. Under Stages, keep Only analyses performed on to draw what you actually ran, and switch off any stage you do not want.
  4. Under Show, choose whether the boxes list the settings (thresholds, resamples) and whether the “Not pass” loops are drawn.
  5. Under Style, add a title if your journal wants one, choose Colour or Greyscale, a palette, or your own colours for each stage.
  6. Click PNG (high resolution) or SVG to save the figure for your manuscript.

The figure is rebuilt from your project every time you open it, so it always matches the settings you report. Greyscale suits journals that print in black and white.

Integrated findings table: PLS-SEM, NCA and fsQCA side by side

When a study combines methods, readers want one table that says what each method found for each condition. The integrated findings table puts the PLS-SEM path, the NCA necessity effect and the fsQCA role of every condition in one row, with a plain interpretation (Sukhov et al., 2023).

  1. In the PLS-SEM analysis, switch on NCA in the analysis settings, choose the outcome and run the model with bootstrapping.
  2. In the PLS-SEM results, open Next steps and choose Create fsQCA analysis…: the construct scores become calibrated sets and the fsQCA runs.
  3. Click Export in the toolbar and choose Integrated findings table (it is also in Next steps).
  4. Choose the PLS-SEM analysis, the outcome and the fsQCA analysis at the top. Copy pastes the table into Word or Excel; CSV saves it.

The three methods answer different questions. PLS-SEM shows the average net effect, NCA whether a condition is a must-have, and fsQCA which combinations are enough. Here performance expectancy is all three, while effort expectancy has no net effect yet still belongs to the recipe that leads to high intention.

Shape path lines like a vector drawing app

The lines of the diagram can be edited as in a vector drawing program such as Inkscape, Illustrator or PowerPoint: structural paths, the lines between constructs and their indicators, and the links of higher-order constructs. Curve them around other elements, give them corners and set their colour, width, dash and arrowhead, so the diagram is ready for a paper.

  1. Click a line to select it. The Line shape bar appears at the left of the canvas.
  2. Click Curve to bend the line smoothly: it gets an anchor with two round curve handles.
  3. Click the selected line anywhere to add another anchor. Drag a square anchor to move it; double-click it to remove it.
  4. Drag a round handle to change the curve. Alt-drag a handle to bend one side only, or Alt-drag a corner anchor to pull out curve handles.
  5. With an anchor selected, Corner switches it between a smooth point and a sharp corner. Straighten removes every anchor.

The format section of the properties panel sets the look of the selected line: its colour, a thicker or thinner line, solid, dashed or dotted, and a filled or open arrowhead. It can also reset the line to the default format.

Right-click a path for Curve path and Straighten path. Undo (Ctrl+Z) reverses any change. The shapes and styles are saved in the project and kept in the exported diagram.

Which test? Let PathLab propose the statistical test

Not sure which test fits your question? In a Statistics analysis, Which test? proposes one from the measurement levels of your variables, the number of groups and whether the measurements are paired, as DATAtab and numiqo do.

  1. Open a Statistics analysis and click Which test? at the right of the Analyses ribbon.
  2. Tick the variables you want to analyse, for example age.
  3. Choose a grouping or second variable if there is one, for example gender, and say whether the variables are repeated measurements of the same cases.
  4. Read the proposal and its reason, then click Create to add the analysis with sensible options already set.
  • Two groups: t-test (Welch shown too) or Mann-Whitney U for ordinal data.
  • Three or more groups: one-way ANOVA with Games-Howell post hoc tests, or Kruskal-Wallis.
  • Repeated measurements: paired t-test, repeated measures ANOVA, Wilcoxon or Friedman.
  • Two categorical variables: chi-square test of a contingency table; ratings of the same cases: kappa.
  • Tick I want to predict an outcome to get a linear, logistic or Poisson regression instead.

The proposal is a starting point. Check the assumptions in the results (normality, equal variances) and switch to the robust or non-parametric version when they fail.
